# Equaliser receive path

`m0lte::ms110d::DfeReceiver` (`src/dfe_receiver.{hpp,cpp}`, the turbo stage in `src/dfe_turbo.cpp`, the MFB block decoder in `src/mfb_block_decoder.{hpp,cpp}`) is the receive side of the equaliser waveforms (WN 1 to 8 and 13): everything the C# `Ms110dDemodulator` does after the preamble detector (docs/dev/preamble-detector.md) has handed over a burst, up to the decoded blocks and the end of the burst. It is ported from M0LTE.Ms110d 0.3.0, step for step. It is internal, like the detector and the WN 0 receiver: not installed and not in the locked API. The public receiver (docs/dev/receiver.md) is built on it.

## What is in it

- `InitializeDfe`: tap counts, lead and ridges per probe length (`eq_params_for_probe_length`), the least-squares solve over the last half preamble super-frame and the first mini-probe, RLS seeded from it with the frame-tied forgetting factor `1 - ln 10 / U`, and the probe statistics the collapse and signal-lost tests measure against. The level gain and the carrier re-fit the C# does first here are already done by the detector, whose event carries the result.
- `ProcessFrame`, per frame: the probe-anchored solve (with the standard probe pattern only, `src/probe_rule.hpp`; the C# `ProbePattern` hypotheses are not ported, issue 68), collapse detection and the fresh re-solve, the phase re-anchor, the carrier trim from the tap rotation (clamped to 3 Hz of the acquired carrier), the fading statistic and its per-burst latch, then the data span: three passes averaged on a static channel, the probe-anchored tap trajectory with RLS on top when fading, or the QAM path. Then soft values, probe timing, the probe presence statistic (with the carrier cancellation) and the signal-lost and signal-absent exits.
- `FinishBlock`: deinterleave, depuncture onto the mother lattice (`depuncture_soft`, the C# `Ms110dPuncture.Depuncture`), tail-biting Viterbi, then the turbo stage, then the EOM scan over the burst's bits and the block limit.
- The turbo stage (`src/dfe_turbo.cpp`), for every mode but QAM16 when the whole block is in the ring: iteration 0 re-equalises on the hard re-encoded decode (`TurboReequalize`), later ones on SISO soft expectations with the code's extrinsics as priors (`TurboReequalizeSoft`), each through `TurboCore`: per frame a channel-shortening feed-forward re-solve on the expected symbols, per-segment main, echo and straddle gains, the scrambler-exact echo span, the noise floor with the per-segment spike pricing, and the chain BCJR. Up to 24 rounds until two decodes agree; no fixed point keeps the first-pass decode. For 8PSK a block with no fixed point goes to the salvage rungs (a label-free frozen-probe re-detection, then the same with the late-lock geometry offered), and every 8PSK block is offered to the MFB decoder, whose decode replaces the chain path's when its evidence is the larger (the C#'s G1d ensemble). Taps and the training rows are saved and restored around it all, and nothing else the tracking uses is touched.
- The MFB block decoder (`src/mfb_block_decoder.*`, the C# `Ms110dMfbBlockDecoder`), which for QAM16 replaces the turbo loop: a per-burst delay-profile scan places a 26 tap window, per block probe anchors, a linear MMSE cold rung, soft then hard cancellation rungs with one gated decision-directed re-fit, to an exact fixed point or the better member of a two-cycle, with the hard-first schedule as a fallback.
- The samples come from the shared front end and ring (`src/rx_stream.hpp`: mixer, matched filter, decimation to the T/2 stream), which the detector and the WN 0 receiver read too (docs/dev/receiver.md).
- The terminate command (`terminate()`, ending the burst with every bit decoded so far, as the C#) and the C#'s instrument options: `has_rls_forgetting_factor` and `rls_forgetting_factor` (the C# `RlsForgettingFactor`), `has_track_ridge` and `track_ridge` (`TrackRidge`), `noise_pricing` (`TurboNsegMode`: spike up, off, two-sided) and `presence_release_off` (`PresenceReleaseOff`; the statistic is still computed). With their defaults nothing changes.

## Where the boundary is

Not here yet:

- The public API, events and options: docs/dev/receiver.md.
- The C#'s diagnostic instruments (channel-truth genie, oracle and truth passes, the probe and pair diagnostics, the consensus and alias-null frozen variants, the W5a seam), which are off in normal use and leave the shipped path bit-identical when off. Only the shipped settings of the frozen pass (pre-cursor chains on, relock on its rung) are ported.
- WN 0, which is `Wn0Receiver` (docs/dev/wn0-receiver.md).

## Using it

`configure(config, storage, decoders, stream)` with caller storage of `storage_bytes(config)` bytes, the caller's decoders (`DfeDecoders`: tail-biting Viterbi and SISO for K 7 and 9, built for `max_input_bits_for(config.max_interleaver)` info bits, and a `BasicChainBcjr<float>` for `max_frame_symbols` symbols and 16 points; each can be built in caller storage, and the K 7 and K 9 pointers may name one decoder set up for the burst's code before `start()`, as the public receiver does) and the shared stream, which must hold `ring_size_for(config.max_interleaver)` samples. Whoever drives the stream calls `on_t2()` after every new T/2 sample, after the detector's; `StandaloneDfeReceiver` carries its own stream and `push_audio()` drives it, returning how many samples it used and stopping after a sample that raised an event. When the detector reports a burst, call `start(dfe_start_from_detection(detection))` before pushing the samples after the detection: the C# initialises the equaliser when a fixed number of samples past the data start have arrived, and the reads there depend on exactly how many have (the interpolator reads zero for a sample not yet received), so the moment matters to the bit. `event()` is then `BlockDecoded` (the block's index, decoded bits, first-pass soft values, what the turbo stage did and its last iterate's soft values, whether the MFB decode was weighed and kept, and the T/2 sample it happened at, all valid until the next push) or `BurstEnded` (reason, blocks, payload length: bits before the EOM, or all decoded bits without one, and the T/2 sample); a block that ends the burst raises both, the block first. `take_event()` moves on. Every event carries its sample position, so tests match them with the C#'s one for one. Bits are delivered a block at a time and the end event only closes the burst; the receiver keeps 31 bits of the burst for the EOM scan and nothing more.

`set_frame_trace()` installs a per-frame hook with the tracking state (the C# `FrameDiagnostics` point), for tests and diagnostics.

`tests/support/rx_driver.{hpp,cpp}` runs the detector and the receiver on one shared stream the way the C# does, for the tests; the public receiver does the same for real.

## Precision

Exactly the C#'s choices, the same in both builds, as in the detector: samples, the ring, the interpolated reads, the equaliser, decisions and soft values are single precision (the C# `Complex32` and `float`, through `src/dsp/complex_f.hpp`, so products round as the C#'s do); the carrier and timing model, the interpolator weights, the probe statistics (gain, MSE, energy and their references), the fading statistic and all of the presence statistic are double. Mixed expressions keep the C#'s types (for example the probe gain is a float divided by an int, then widened). The only `real` is inside the equaliser, which keeps its running sums in `real` (docs/dev/equaliser.md), so the single build differs from the C# only there. `-ffp-contract=off` keeps compiler fusing out.

The turbo stage follows the same rule. Its sums and gains are float as in the C#; the SISO and Viterbi decoders are float in both builds (docs/dev/soft-decoders.md); and the chain BCJR is `BasicChainBcjr<float>`, the C#'s precision, in both builds. The BCJR class is a template on its scalar for that: `ChainBcjr` remains the `real` instance soft-decoders.md describes. A double BCJR was tried first. It differs from the C#'s float one by rounding only, but on a block of noise (the padding after a burst without an EOM) that was enough to turn two of 214 reference blocks from reverted to converged, so the turbo stage would not be the C#'s. With the float BCJR the default build reproduces the C# bit for bit through every turbo round. The MFB decoder is double precision in both builds, as the C#'s System.Numerics.Complex is; `NetComplex` writes out .NET's operations (the products in its order, Smith's division, the overflow-safe magnitude) so the arithmetic is the same.

## Memory

Nothing allocates after `configure` and nothing throws, through every turbo, salvage and MFB path (tested). The object is 122912 bytes: fixed scratch for 64 taps and 256 data symbols, the turbo stage's per-frame arrays (the largest is the chain BCJR's priors, 16 points by 256 symbols) and the MFB decoder's 26 by 26 and 37 by 37 complex matrices. The caller's block holds the equaliser's block (the T/2 ring is the shared stream's) (sized for the largest, K = 48), the block's soft values and decode buffers, the presence statistic's DFT tables, the turbo stage's block buffers (soft values, SISO buffers, expected symbols) and the MFB decoder's (its block of samples and reconstruction, decisions and projections, all double complex):

| Longest interleaver accepted | Ring needed (T/2 samples) | Storage | Decoders for (info bits) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ultra-short | 4096 | 295704 bytes | 768 |
| Short | 8192 | 683560 bytes | 3072 |
| Medium | 16384 | 2234968 bytes | 12288 |
| Long | 65536 (the C#'s) | 8440600 bytes | 49152 |

The ring holds a whole interleaver block plus the probe after it and the last preamble super-frame, because the turbo pass re-reads the block at its end (`block_samples_resident` is the C#'s backstop and always holds for this sizing). The C# always uses 65536. The ring size never changes a result: every read is of a sample still in the ring (asserted in debug builds), so the shared ring, which is at least the detector's 16384, reads the same. The decoders' own tables come on top (the SISO keeps the forward metrics for the whole block, docs/dev/soft-decoders.md). Ways to shrink for a microcontroller (issue 44): size the equaliser and the MFB decoder for the waveforms actually accepted (a BPSK-only build needs no MFB storage at all, about half the short-interleaver block), compute the DFT table for the burst's probe length only, window the block buffers, and keep the MFB's buffers in single precision (an accepted deviation with its test).

## Tests and agreement with the C#

`tests/dfe_receiver_test.cpp` (the first pass, turbo off) and `tests/dfe_turbo_test.cpp` (the shipped receiver), against dumps from the real 0.3.0 demodulator made by `tools/csharp-vectors`: `RxDump.cs` (`rx`, `rx-awgn`) and `TurboDump.cs` (`rx-turbo`, `rx-corpus`). Private fields are read by reflection at the C#'s `FrameDiagnostics` point, the presence statistic comes from `PresenceTraced`, the soft values from `FirstPassBlockLlrs` and `TurboBlockLlrs`, the decoded bits from `BlockDecoded` and what the turbo stage did from the public counters. Together they are about 10 MiB with the corpus audio, so none is committed: `scripts/generate-rx-dumps.sh [cache-dir]` writes `csharp-rx.txt`, `csharp-rx-awgn.txt`, `csharp-rx-turbo.txt` and `rx-corpus.{txt,i16le}` to `build/_reference` from the reference files there (a stamp of the tool sources and the pin skips a rerun, about three minutes otherwise), CMake's `MS110D_RX_DUMP_DIR` points the tests at them, and the CI reference job generates them (cached) and runs the tests in both precisions. The tests skip without them, and fail when `MS110D_REQUIRE_REFERENCE` is set.

**The probe pattern in the comparisons (issue 68).** The C# 0.3.0 receiver and the C++ receiver both expect the standard pattern only, so every comparison feeds both the audio the C# transmitter makes (the reference cases' audio, corpus bursts from the plain modulator) and nothing is forced on the C# side. The hash of the tracking state does not include any pattern evidence.

The first pass alone (turbo off, `dfe_receiver_test.cpp`):

- **Clean, every WN 1 to 5 ultra-short and short reference case (41).** Frame by frame: the same frames at the same input sample, the same fading, latch, fresh-solve and bad-probe decisions, and the carrier, timing, probe references, MSE, fade floor, presence statistics and tap energy; the decoded bits exactly (they equal the transmitted payload, and the C#'s with turbo on); the first-pass soft values; the end of the burst (reason, blocks, payload length, sample). Measured: the default build reproduces the C# bit for bit, every value difference 0 and the hash of the taps after every one of the 870 frames equal; the single build is within 8.3e-7 (relative) everywhere, with every decision and decoded bit the same.
- **Seeded AWGN, 212 bursts.** WN 1 to 5 short, K 7, twenty trials at each of two SNRs per waveform, from where the first pass fails every burst (WN 1 at -9 dB) to where it decodes all of them; plus twelve cut bursts (WN 2 and 4, the signal stops half way and 20 s of noise follow, a steady tone on a probe line in half of them), which end in the signal-lost exit (11) and the signal-absent exit (1) and exercise the presence statistic's carrier cancellation. The noise generator is repeated in the test, so no audio is stored. Per block: the decoded bits exactly, the sign of every soft value (346624 of them) exactly, the hash of the soft values' bits, the sum of their magnitudes, the T/2 sample; per burst the frame count, the end (reason, blocks, payload, sample) and the final tracking state. Measured: identical in the default build (all 438 soft value hashes equal); in the single build, signs, decoded bits and ends identical, magnitudes within 2.2e-7, tracking state within 1.7e-5.
- The bounds in the test (1e-4 relative on values, 1e-8 on the carrier increment, 1e-3 rad on the phase) are far above what is measured, to leave room for another machine's libm, whose last bits of sine and cosine move the detector's carrier by about 1e-6 Hz (docs/dev/preamble-detector.md). Decisions, decoded bits, signs, frame and sample positions are exact.

The shipped receiver, turbo stage and MFB decoder included (`dfe_turbo_test.cpp`). Per burst: the frame count; per block, the T/2 sample, what the turbo stage did (converged, salvaged, reverted, aborted, skipped) and whether the MFB decode was weighed and kept, a hash over every frame's tracking state (carrier, timing, references, MSE, fade floor, presence, the taps, the latch, and the fade detector's own flag and frames-since-excursion counter), the frozen pass's late-lock offers and adoptions, the first-pass soft values (hash of the float bits, signs, magnitudes), the last turbo iterate's soft values (hash, every sign, magnitudes clipped at 30, since on a clean signal they saturate at 1e6 and more), the decoded bits; and the end and payload.

- **Every WN 1 to 8 and 13 reference case (112), every interleaver.** The default build: everything bit for bit, every frame state hash (214 blocks) and every turbo soft value (500224) identical, 212 blocks converged and 2 reverted (on a burst without an EOM, the block made of the padding after it), 19 MFB offers; the 109 bursts that end at their EOM decode their payload. Two have no EOM and one has a one super-frame preamble, which neither the C# nor this acquires.
- **The corpus, 166 bursts** made fresh by the tool through M0LTE.HfChannel and quantised to 16 bit audio (the C# ran on the same quantised samples):
  - the Poor channel on every mode, ultra-short and short, at the AWGN mask plus 8 and 12 dB, and the medium interleaver for WN 5 and up;
  - Moderate (two paths 1 ms apart, 0.5 Hz) and a shifted Poor (the second path 6 Hz off the first) with the carrier 20 Hz off either way, three blocks each;
  - a carrier drifting at 4 Hz per second either way (a Hilbert-transform frequency ramp in the tool, since a fixed offset is taken out at acquisition);
  - fading on, off and on again (Poor, then a static direct path, then Poor, a third of a 24 block burst each) on WN 1 to 4, so the fade detector enters, leaves and enters again;
  - eight WN 7 Poor bursts, found by search, whose blocks the 8PSK salvage saves on its second, late-lock rung (one refusing the offer, one taking one of two);
  - the standard probe pattern, sent by the C# modulator (the only pattern it has since 0.3.0), on every mode with the ultra-short, short and medium interleavers clean and short in noise, and three bursts long enough to reach the probe only the standard rule shifts (frame L - 2, L the long interleaver's frames: WN 1 short over 16 blocks, WN 3 medium over 4, WN 5 short over 16);
  - seven bursts found by search (on a spare machine, over a few thousand candidates) for the boundaries of two constants: fade detector excursions with its counter at exactly 24 (enters; a 23 frame window would not) and 25 (does not; a 25 frame window would), and late-lock offers whose floor ratio sits either side of the 0.5 adoption margin (0.455, 0.516, 0.552).

  The default build reproduces the C# bit for bit on every burst: 668 frame state hashes, 832640 turbo soft values, 599 converged, 55 reverted and 14 salvaged blocks (7 on the late-lock rung; 19 offers, 13 adopted), 78 MFB offers and 18 selections, the same payloads right (130 of 166). What the corpus exercises is checked too: 152 frames at the 3 Hz carrier clamp, 83 entries to and 21 exits from fading with 17 re-entries, 3496 fade floor recoveries, the late-lock salvages, refused and taken offers, MFB selections and reverts, and the long standard bursts reaching frame L - 2. The whole corpus also runs with tracing off inside an allocation scope: no allocation, with reverts, salvages, late-lock offers and MFB selections among it.
- **Mutations.** Because the default build is held to every frame state hash (which includes the fade detector's flag and counter) and to every block's turbo outcome and late-lock counts, a change to a tracking constant fails the test. Checked by applying each of these to the C++ alone and running the corpus comparison; each one fails it: the fade detector's enter window 24 changed to 2, 12, 23, 25 or 48, its exit 32 to 4, 31 or 33, the frame number computed without the block index, the late-lock rung disabled, and its 0.5 adoption margin changed to 0.4, 0.6 or 1.0.
- **The standard probe pattern, end to end.** Every burst the tool sends with the ultra-short, short and medium interleavers carries the standard pattern, and the "std-" bursts (clean or mild noise, 39 of them) must decode their payload, in both builds, with the C++ expecting that pattern from the first frame (including the two WN 1 and WN 2 ultra-short bursts of two blocks, where the C# alone never became sure of the pattern: its two disagreeing probes sum to 0.40 against the 0.5 it needs).
- **The single build.** It keeps the equaliser's running sums in float where the C# uses double, and on a hard fading or drifting burst that can move the tracking and so the first pass and the turbo outcomes; how far depends on the machine's libm too. There the test counts the differences and holds the single build to two bounds. First, the C#'s decoding success: at least as many payloads right, give or take one. Second, the number of bursts that take a different path: none of the 112 clean reference cases, and at most 24 of the 166 corpus bursts, the worst measured plus a small margin (the CI reference job prints the tally). The divergence comes from float sums tipping marginal fading blocks, so it stays a minority, and a broken single build would leave most bursts. Measured: the clean cases identical in every decision; in the corpus 17 bursts (25 blocks) take a different path on one machine, 19 (30 blocks) on the CI runner and 20 (32 blocks) on an older CPU without fused multiply-add, whose libm takes other paths, and the single build decodes exactly as many payloads right as the C# (130 of 166). All 39 "std-" bursts decode in the single build too.

Without reference files:

- The C++ transmitter into the C++ receiver, turbo on: every equaliser mode with the ultra-short and short interleavers, K 7 and 9, at block-edge payload lengths including an EOM whose last bit falls in the next block (180 bursts); the medium interleaver on every mode and the long one on WN 3, 7 and 8 (12 bursts, every block converging); and in noise 3 dB above the C# dump's upper points for WN 1 to 5 and above the Table D-LXIV AWGN mask for the rest (72 bursts). All decode.
- Results independent of the chunk size (1 to 100000 samples), zero allocation over whole bursts of WN 1, 6, 7 near its mask and 8 (the turbo loop, the 8PSK ensemble and the QAM16 MFB decoder), storage, configuration and refusals, `depuncture_soft` against the puncture map for every rate, and the probe rule (`probe_is_shifted`).

Because the default build's decisions are identical on every burst, the receive difference against the C# on these signals is zero, far inside the plan's 0.5 dB.
